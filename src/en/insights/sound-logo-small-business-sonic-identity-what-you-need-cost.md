A sound logo is to the ear what the graphic logo is to the eye: a short sound or melody, always the same, that people associate with the brand even without looking at the screen. For a small company it makes sense in some specific cases, and its cost depends more on the rights than on the seconds of music.

<div class="riquadro">

**The short answer.** A sound logo is a 2-5 second sound tied to the brand and reused in all videos, commercials and social content. It matters most for brands that address the public and advertise often, because it sticks through repetition. The cost has three parts: the work, the idea and the usage rights, which change a lot depending on whether the company buys a full assignment or a licence.

</div>

## Logo with sound, sound logo, sonic identity

- **A logo with sound** is the animated logo at the end of a commercial, with a sound designed for that video. It is part of the sound design of the project.
- **A sound logo** is made to last: the same sound accompanies the logo in all future videos, in radio spots and on social media, like the colours and the typeface.
- **Sonic identity** is the whole system: sound logo, video music, type of voice, app sounds. The sound logo is its centre.

## Who really needs one

A sound becomes recognisable only if it is heard many times.

- It makes sense for a brand that sells to the public and runs advertising or social campaigns with some frequency.
- For a small brand that wants to become known it gives recognisability, and sets it apart from those using the same library music as everyone else.
- For a company that makes one video a year, a sound logo is superfluous.

## What a good sound logo must do

- **Be short**: usually 2-5 seconds, the length of the logo animation.
- **Be recognisable**: a few notes, a timbre, a sonic gesture that belongs only to that brand.
- **Work without the picture**: on the radio, or in a video watched out of the corner of the eye.
- **Work on a phone**: small speakers do not reproduce bass, and a logo built on a bass hit almost disappears.
- **Sit under different music and voices**, in every commercial.
- **Fit the brand**: a fast metallic sound tells the story of a different company than a warm piano timbre.

## How the work goes

1. **The brief**: who the brand is, who it speaks to, the animated logo, where the sound will be used. How to write it is covered in the [guide to the sound design brief](/en/insights/sound-design-brief-commercial-video-what-to-include/).
2. **The proposals**: a few different directions, edited onto the animated logo.
3. **The choice** of one direction and **the refinements**.
4. **The tests** on a phone, computer speakers and TV.
5. **The delivery**: main version, short and long, versions per channel with the right levels (they are in the [guide to LUFS by destination](/en/insights/what-lufs-to-deliver-a-video-instagram-youtube-tv-cinema/)) and separate tracks, to re-edit the logo under different music.

## What drives the cost

The sound logo has its own item in the quote, separate from the sound design of the video in which it first appears:

- **the work**: proposals, refinements, versions, tests, final files;
- **the idea**: an original sound that the brand will use for years;
- **the usage rights**: duration, channels, exclusivity. This is the part that varies most from one company to another.

That is why a sound logo costs more than a logo with sound inside a single video, even if it lasts the same number of seconds.

## Assignment or licence: who owns the sound

In Italy the author holds the rights to a work from the moment it is created (law 633/1941).

- **Assignment of economic exploitation rights** (the buy out): the company becomes the owner and uses the sound as it likes, anywhere and forever. It is the broadest and most expensive option.
- **Licence of use**: the company uses it within the agreed limits (perpetual or renewable, on some channels or all, exclusive or not); the rights stay with the author.
- The moral right to be recognised as the author cannot be transferred.
- The transfer of rights must be proven in writing: the agreement is always written down, with duration, channels and exclusivity.
- A sound logo can also be registered as a sound mark, with the Italian Patent and Trademark Office or with the EUIPO for the whole European Union, by filing the audio file.

## What to prepare before asking for a quote

- the animated logo, even a provisional one, or its length;
- where the sound will be used and how often: commercials, social media, radio, trade fairs, app;
- two or three references of sounds or music that feel close to the brand, and one that feels far from it;
- what kind of rights you need: full assignment or licence, for how long, on which channels;
- the dates: when you need the proposals and when the final files.

You do not need an answer for every point: if it feels complicated, a phone call is enough and we can talk it through.
