The cost of the audio for a commercial depends on what sits inside the word "audio": sometimes it is just a mix of a voice and music that already exist, sometimes it is casting and recording the voice, searching for the music, sound design, mixing and a series of versions for different channels. That is why a serious quote comes after a few details about the project, and the more complete they are, the more likely the first quote is also the final one.

## The information needed

1. **What type of video it is**: commercial, brand video, animation, social media video.
2. **Where it goes**: TV, radio, web, social media, cinema, trade fair. Each destination has its own delivery specs, and therefore its own versions.
3. **How many versions**: lengths (30, 20, 15 seconds), different cuts, languages, partly different scripts.
4. **The length**, even approximate.
5. **The final or rough edit**, if there is one already.
6. **References or brief**: one or more reference videos are the quickest way to understand the intentions. How to write one is in the [guide to the sound design brief](/en/insights/sound-design-brief-commercial-video-what-to-include/).
7. **The music**: is it already there, does it need to be found, or composed? What genre?
8. **The voice**: voice actor or AI voice, and whether the script is final.
9. **The audio material** available: production sound, effects, music, voices already recorded.

The reach of the campaign also matters: where it will air, for how long, how much weight it carries for the brand. This information guides the work and affects the music and voice licences.

## The items in a quote

The items vary a lot from project to project. The most common ones:

- **voice casting**: a few options on an excerpt of the script, to choose from;
- **recording or directing the voice actor**, or editing the AI voice, for each version;
- **music search and editing** to the different lengths;
- **sound design**: sound effects on actions, products, animations, transitions;
- **mix and master** for each destination;
- **additional versions**: other lengths, other channels, other languages.

Sound design often appears as a separate item, sometimes optional, so whoever receives the quote can see what it adds and decide.

## What changes a quote

The variable that most often changes a quote after it is first sent is the **number of versions and formats**: two TV spots of different lengths that become one, a radio version that gets added, a length that changes. Each version has its own voice editing, its own music edit and its own mix. The second reason is the **starting material**: music chosen by the client that turns out to need a search, an edit that changes after the audio has been produced.

## Music and voice: who provides what

- **Library music**: searching and editing it to the versions is work; the licence is a separate cost. The licence can be bought by whoever does the audio, who puts it on the invoice, or directly by the client, given the exact track and the type of licence to buy. Coverage differs between web, TV and radio: the details are in the [guide to music for a radio ad](/en/insights/music-for-radio-ad-licensing-artlist-epidemic-sound/) and in the one on [music for corporate videos](/en/insights/music-for-corporate-video-royalty-free-library-custom-licensing-ads/).
- **Voice**: a voice actor has their own fee and usage rights, tied to the channels and length of the campaign, plus the markup of whoever handles the editing; an AI voice has a commercial use licence, which has to cover broadcast. The differences are in the [guide on AI voice and voice actors](/en/insights/ai-voice-or-voice-actor-commercial-video-ai-act/).

## Revisions

A number of included revision rounds, usually three, written into a clause, and an hourly rate beyond those. In practice they are rarely exceeded: the clause keeps requests concrete and brings the job to a close. Two points worth putting in writing: a change to the script after the voice has been approved counts as a revision round, and major changes to the edit after the audio has been delivered are new work, to be assessed separately.

## Deliverables

Each channel has its own specs. For Italian TV, AGCOM resolution 219/09/CSP requires -24 LUFS with true peak at -2 dBTP; for radio a single master is usually enough, with dedicated versions if a station asks for different specs; web and social media have different values again. All the levels are in the [guide to LUFS by destination](/en/insights/what-lufs-to-deliver-a-video-instagram-youtube-tv-cinema/). Knowing from the start where the commercial will go avoids redoing mixes later.

## What sound design adds

A commercial with music only, or with music and voice, works. A commercial where the sound effects are also crafted (the gesture, the product, the transition, the environment) feels more present, reads more clearly and is remembered more: sound guides the eye and gives weight to the images. It is the part that changes the perception of the commercial the most compared to how much it weighs on the budget.

## What to send for a quote

The nine items from the list at the top, even in rough form, plus the dates: when the first version is needed for approvals and when the masters are due. With these, the quote is accurate on the first round.
